Im Dickicht

To the artists →View works →

We didn't want pretty forests.

We wanted images in which the tangled and impenetrable conceals something – photos that make you sense that something is off before you know what.

Petra van Husen, curator of the exhibition

The first open call at weissraum. The jury chose from nearly 200 submissions. The jury members are themselves represented with works.

When we announced our first open call in June, this was the sentence everything was built around. We didn't want pretty forests. We wanted images in which the tangled and impenetrable conceals something – photos that make you sense that something is off before you know what.

An open call is not something a small exhibition space can take for granted. You send a question out into the world and don't know whether anyone will answer. People did answer. From several countries, some from far away.

And they submitted something we hadn't expected: hardly any landscape, but a great deal of staging. Constructed scenes in the undergrowth. People hiding – or wanting to be found. In the end, nearly 200 submissions lay on the table.

The selection was made by a jury of three: Petra van Husen, Andreas Koridass and Thomas Weikert. That all three are also represented in the exhibition with their own work is deliberate: weissraum is an atelier, not a neutral art space. We are putting ourselves to the same question we asked.

The selection was hard for us. There was far more worth seeing and showing than we can show. What connects the selected works: they demand a second look, sometimes a third. In a time when images are consumed in fractions of a second, that is almost an imposition – and precisely for that reason worth the attempt.

The thicket is not a motif. It is a stage.
